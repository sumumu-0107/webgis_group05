

1. Data Collection and Study Area Preparation
The PM2.5 and Crops analysis started with the collection of CAMS reanalysis data, ESRI Land Cover data, WorldPop population data, and GAUL Level 2 administrative boundaries for Greece. The CAMS dataset provided monthly PM2.5 concentration raster layers, while the land cover data were used to identify crop area transitions between 2021 and 2023. The population raster from WorldPop was used to evaluate population exposure to different PM2.5 concentration classes. Before any spatial analysis, all datasets were clipped to the Greece study area to reduce file size and ensure that all layers represented the same geographic extent. This step was important because the later overlay, zonal statistics, and bivariate mapping required all input layers to be spatially consistent.

2. Coordinate Reference System and Raster Alignment
After importing the datasets into QGIS, the coordinate reference systems of all layers were checked and harmonized. The pollutant rasters, land cover rasters, population raster, and administrative boundary layers needed to be aligned in the same spatial framework. Reprojection and clipping tools were used where necessary to make sure that the layers overlapped correctly. This step prevented errors during raster calculation and zonal statistics, especially because the analysis combined raster data with vector administrative zones. A consistent CRS and spatial extent also ensured that the exported maps could be properly integrated into the web mapping framework later.

3. December 2023 PM2.5 Monthly Mean Aggregation
To account for data that required user aggregation, the hourly raw NetCDF files for December 2023 downloaded from the Copernicus Atmosphere Data Store (ADS) were processed. In the QGIS Raster Calculator, a temporal mean aggregation was executed across all operational sub-bands representing each hour to yield the average monthly layer. The output was clipped using the Greek mask layer to produce the standard raster layer named Greece_CAMS_pm2p5_2023_12.tif.

4. Annual Mean PM2.5 Raster Generation (2021 and 2023)
The annual average concentration of PM2.5 was computed for both 2021 and 2023. The pre-aggregated monthly layers provided by the tutors (January 2021 to December 2021, and January 2023 to November 2023) were loaded along with the newly generated December 2023 layer. Using the Raster Calculator, the twelve monthly bands for each year were summed and divided by 12: (Band_01 + Band_02 + ... + Band_12) / 12. The resulting annual average rasters were saved as Greece_average_pm2p5_2021.tif and Greece_average_pm2p5_2023.tif.

5. PM2.5 Concentration Reclassification
The 2023 PM2.5 annual mean dataset was converted from continuous values into five discrete concentration classes based on health guidelines. The "Reclassify by Table" module was configured using standard breaks derived from the European Environment Agency (EEA) and EU health guidelines, strictly reflecting the target annual limit value of 25 μg/m³ for PM2.5. The output layer, showing the geographic distribution of health hazard tiers from Class 1 to Class 5, was saved as Greece_pm2p5_concentration_map_2023.tif.

6. PM2.5 Annual Mean Aggregation Change (AMAC)
To quantify the absolute variation of particulate matter concentration over time, an atmospheric trend analysis was conducted. The 2021 annual mean layer was subtracted from the 2023 annual mean layer inside the Raster Calculator: "Greece_average_pm2p5_2023@1" - "Greece_average_pm2p5_2021@1". The resulting layer was exported as Greece_pm2p5_2021_2023_AMAC_map.tif, accompanied by a customized symbology descriptor file named Greece_pm2p5_2021_2023_AMAC_map.qml to clearly highlight areas of particulate concentration increase and decrease.

7. Land Cover Change Mapping and Resampling
The multi-temporal ESRI 10m Annual Land Cover datasets for 2021 and 2023 were processed to analyze agricultural dynamics. To perform change detection against the coarser resolution of the CAMS atmospheric grid, the "Align Rasters" tool was implemented. The land cover layers were upscaled using the Nearest Neighbor resampling approach to preserve the discrete integer values of the original classes. The 2021 classification layer was multiplied by 100 and added to the 2023 layer inside the Raster Calculator to generate a transition map. This map isolated stable areas and conversion trajectories, with a specific focus on identifying the stability, gain, and loss of Crops (Class 5). The layer was saved as Greece_LCC_2021_2023.tif.

8. Zonal Statistics for PM2.5 Change across Crop Transition Zones
To evaluate potential correlations between agricultural activities and particulate trends, a land-air feedback assessment was carried out. The transition categories within the crop layer were converted from raster cells into vector zones. The "Zonal Statistics" tool was executed to extract the exact Mean, Minimum, and Maximum values from the PM2.5 AMAC map within each crop transition zone (Stable Crops, Crop Gain, and Crop Loss). The spatial statistical vector layer was exported as Greece_pm2p5_zonal_statistics_2021_2023.gpkg, providing the mathematical data needed to build multi-bar comparative charts for the website.

9. Population Reclassification for Exposure Indexing
Demographic data from WorldPop were prepared to serve as the population density parameter for exposure mapping. The 2023 population count raster for Greece was reclassified using the "Reclassify by Table" tool. Continuous population numbers per pixel were grouped into uniform demographic classes based on natural breaks or quintiles to prepare a standardized index for the subsequent bivariate analysis.

10. Bivariate Overlay for PM2.5 and Population Exposure
To map the cross-analysis parameter combining particulate hazard and human presence, a bivariate overlay was performed. The reclassified PM2.5 concentration map and the reclassified population density map were combined. In the Field Calculator, the categorical attributes were combined using a matrix string formula: "pol_class_max" * 10 + "pop_class_median". This created unique two-digit codes (ranging from 11 to 55) for each polygon to enable a 5×5 legend structure. The vector layer containing these bivariate codes was saved as Greece_pm2p5_2023_bivariate.gpkg.

11. Dissolving Risk Layers and Calculating Exposure Proportions
To prepare statistical data for human risk assessment, the vectorized exposure was simplified. The vector geometry was aggregated using the "Dissolve" tool based on the maximum pollution attributes (pol_class_max) to generate a streamlined dataset with a maximum of 5 categorical features. The "Zonal Statistics" tool was run against the underlying 2023 WorldPop raster to compute the cumulative sum of exposed individuals (pop_sum) within each hazard level. The output was saved as Greece_pm2p5_2023_chart.gpkg, which was used to create a pie chart showing the percentage of the Greek population exposed to different PM2.5 concentration classes.

12. Map Layout Export for Website Results
After all PM2.5 and crop analysis layers were produced, the main maps were exported using QGIS Layout. Each static map was designed with a map title, legend, scale bar, and north arrow to meet standard cartographic requirements. The exported PM2.5 maps included the December 2023 map, annual average map, concentration class map, annual mean aggregation change map, crop area change map, and bivariate exposure map. The bar chart and pie chart were exported separately as statistical graphics. All images were saved with short English filenames so that they could be easily referenced in the website code.

13. GeoServer Publishing and WebGIS Preparation
The final PM2.5 layers were prepared for publication in GeoServer. The key layers included the PM2.5 concentration class raster, PM2.5 annual mean aggregation change raster, crop transition layer, and PM2.5–population bivariate vector layer. Appropriate styles were applied before publishing, especially the predefined 5×5 bivariate style required for the exposure map. After publication, WMS links were tested and recorded for use in the Leaflet/OpenLayers WebGIS page. This made it possible to display the PM2.5 results interactively on the website, alongside the static maps and written interpretation.
