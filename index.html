About This Project

This project analyzes air quality conditions in Greece and presents the results through a WebGIS platform. The study integrates pollutant concentration data, land cover change, population distribution and administrative boundaries to assess both spatial pollution patterns and population exposure.

01

Data Collection

The analysis uses CAMS pollutant rasters, ESRI Land Cover data, WorldPop population data and GAUL Level 2 administrative boundaries. These datasets provide the basis for pollutant mapping, land cover transition analysis and exposure assessment.

02

Spatial Analysis

Raster clipping, reprojection, annual aggregation, reclassification, raster calculation and zonal statistics were used to process the air quality and land cover datasets. These steps convert raw spatial data into interpretable maps and charts.

03

Exposure Assessment

Bivariate mapping combines pollutant concentration classes with population classes. This method helps identify areas where high pollution levels overlap with dense population, indicating higher exposure risk.

Data and Tools

Datasets and Processing Environment

The project combines satellite-derived pollutant data, land cover products, population grids and administrative boundaries. QGIS was used for spatial processing and layout design, while GeoServer and Leaflet were prepared for WebGIS publication.

CAMS Reanalysis

ESRI Land Cover

WorldPop

Population raster data used to evaluate population exposure to pollutant concentration classes.

GAUL Level 2

Administrative boundaries used for zonal statistics and bivariate exposure mapping.

QGIS

Main GIS platform for clipping, reprojection, raster calculation, classification and map layouts.

GeoServer + Leaflet

GeoServer provides WMS services, while Leaflet is used to build the interactive WebGIS interface.

Key Findings

The NO₂ results show that higher concentrations are mainly concentrated around urban and transport-related areas, especially near Athens and parts of northern Greece. Built area transitions are spatially scattered, while NO₂ change is uneven across the country. The bivariate exposure result shows that population distribution strongly affects the interpretation of air quality risk.

Methodology

Workflow

The workflow explains how raw pollutant, land cover and population datasets were processed into final maps, charts and WebGIS layers. Each step includes the main operation, the tool used in QGIS, and the purpose of the processing.

1. Data Collection and Study Area Preparation

The NO₂ analysis started with CAMS reanalysis data, ESRI Land Cover data, WorldPop population data, and GAUL Level 2 administrative boundaries for Greece. CAMS provided monthly NO₂ raster layers, while land cover data were used to identify built area transitions between 2021 and 2023. WorldPop was used to evaluate population exposure to NO₂ concentration classes. All datasets were clipped to the Greece study area to reduce file size and ensure spatial consistency.

2. CRS Checking and Raster Alignment

After importing the datasets into QGIS, the coordinate reference systems and spatial extents were checked. The pollutant rasters, land cover rasters, population raster and administrative boundary layers needed to overlap correctly before raster calculation and zonal statistics. Reprojection and clipping tools were used where necessary. This step prevented misalignment errors and ensured that the final maps could be displayed correctly in QGIS Layout and GeoServer.

3. Monthly and Annual NO₂ Processing

The December 2023 NO₂ raster was clipped to Greece and visualized as a monthly concentration map. Then, the 2023 annual average NO₂ concentration map was calculated from monthly CAMS raster layers. The annual average map smooths monthly variation and provides a more stable representation of NO₂ distribution across the country. This annual mean layer was later used for concentration classification and exposure analysis.

4. NO₂ Concentration Classification

The annual average NO₂ map was reclassified into concentration classes using the lab air quality thresholds. In Greece, the visible classes are mainly Class 1, Class 2 and Class 3. Reclassification converted the continuous concentration raster into a categorical layer, making the spatial pattern easier to interpret. This classified pollutant layer was also required for the NO₂–population bivariate map.

5. Built Area Transition Extraction

Land cover change was calculated by comparing ESRI Land Cover rasters from 2021 and 2023. Because the NO₂ module focuses on urban-related pollution, the built area transition classes were extracted from the full land cover change raster. Three transition categories were used: Stable Built Area, Built Area Gain and Built Area Loss. This transition layer was used as the zone layer for calculating NO₂ change statistics.

6. NO₂ Change and Zonal Statistics

The NO₂ Annual Mean Aggregation Change map was produced by subtracting the 2021 annual mean NO₂ layer from the 2023 annual mean NO₂ layer. Positive values indicate NO₂ increase, while negative values indicate decrease. Zonal statistics were calculated using the built area transition layer as zones and the NO₂ change raster as the value layer. Mean, minimum and maximum change values were then visualized in a bar chart.

7. Population Exposure and Bivariate Mapping

WorldPop 2023 population data were classified into five population classes. GAUL Level 2 administrative zones were then used to summarize the median population class and maximum NO₂ concentration class. A new bivariate field was calculated using the formula pol_class_max × 10 + pop_class_median. The predefined 5×5 bivariate style was applied to visualize the overlap between NO₂ concentration and population exposure.

8. GeoServer and WebGIS Preparation

The final NO₂ layers were prepared for GeoServer publication, including monthly NO₂, annual average NO₂, concentration classes, AMAC, built area change, land cover change and bivariate exposure layers. The local GeoServer preview confirmed that the layers could be served through WMS. In the final website, these WMS layers will be connected to the interactive WebGIS after publication on the online GeoServer.

1. PM2.5 Data Collection and Study Area Preparation

The PM2.5 analysis started with CAMS reanalysis data, ESRI Land Cover, WorldPop population data and GAUL Level 2 administrative boundaries. Monthly PM2.5 rasters were clipped to the Greece study area to ensure consistent extent for later raster calculations, classification and exposure analysis.

2. PM2.5 CRS Checking and Raster Alignment

All pollutant, land cover, population and boundary layers were checked for coordinate reference system consistency and spatial alignment. Reprojection and clipping were used when necessary to avoid overlay errors in raster calculations and zonal statistics.

3. December 2023 PM2.5 Monthly Mean Aggregation

Hourly December 2023 PM2.5 data from the Copernicus ADS were aggregated in QGIS using the Raster Calculator. The hourly bands were averaged to produce a single December 2023 PM2.5 layer clipped to the Greece mask.

4. Annual Mean PM2.5 Raster Generation

The 2021 and 2023 annual average PM2.5 rasters were generated by averaging each year's twelve monthly layers. The arithmetic mean of January through December produced the yearly concentration products used for comparison and classification.

5. PM2.5 Concentration Reclassification

The 2023 annual PM2.5 mean raster was reclassified into five concentration classes using EU health guideline thresholds. This discrete class layer made the spatial hazard pattern easier to interpret and supported the subsequent exposure mapping.

6. PM2.5 Annual Mean Aggregation Change

The 2023 annual PM2.5 layer was subtracted from the 2021 annual PM2.5 layer to create an AMAC map. The resulting change raster highlights where PM2.5 concentration increased or decreased between the two years.

7. Land Cover Change Mapping and Resampling

The 2021 and 2023 ESRI land cover datasets were aligned to the CAMS grid and used to generate a crop area transition map. Nearest-neighbor resampling preserved the discrete land cover classes while enabling comparison with the coarser pollutant raster.

8. Zonal Statistics for PM2.5 Change by Crop Transition

Crop transition zones were converted to vector form and zonal statistics were computed for the PM2.5 AMAC raster. Mean, minimum and maximum PM2.5 change values were extracted for Stable Crop Area, Crop Gain and Crop Loss.

9. Population Reclassification for Exposure Indexing

The 2023 WorldPop population raster was reclassified into demographic classes to create a standardized population index. This prepared population data for the PM2.5 bivariate exposure analysis.

10. Bivariate Overlay for PM2.5 and Population

The classified PM2.5 layer and reclassified population layer were combined to produce a 5×5 bivariate exposure map. Unique two-digit codes were created to represent pollutant class and population class combinations.

11. Exposure Dissolve and Chart Preparation

The PM2.5 exposure vector was dissolved by pollution class and zonal statistics were used to compute population exposure proportions. This data supported charts showing the share of population exposed to each PM2.5 concentration class.

Spatial Outputs

Results

The results section presents the final pollutant maps and charts generated from the analysis. The interpretation focuses on spatial concentration patterns, annual change, built area transition statistics and population exposure.

NO₂ Concentration in Greece, December 2023

The December 2023 NO₂ map shows that concentration values were unevenly distributed across Greece. Higher values mainly appear around major urban and transport-related regions, especially around the Athens metropolitan area and parts of northern Greece. In contrast, many island and rural regions show lower values. This monthly map provides a snapshot of short-term spatial variation before the annual average calculation.

Annual Average NO₂ Concentration in Greece, 2023

The annual average NO₂ map highlights the stable spatial pattern of NO₂ concentration across Greece in 2023. Higher values are concentrated around urbanized areas, while lower values are distributed across rural and island regions. Compared with the December map, the annual average smooths monthly fluctuations and is more suitable for classification and population exposure analysis.

NO₂ Concentration Classes in Greece, 2023

The concentration class map divides annual average NO₂ into air quality classes. Greece mainly contains Class 1, Class 2 and Class 3 values, while Class 4 and Class 5 do not appear in the final map. Most of the country falls into Class 1, indicating relatively low NO₂ concentration levels. Class 2 and Class 3 areas are more limited and are mainly associated with urban regions.

NO₂ Annual Mean Aggregation Change, 2021–2023

The NO₂ AMAC map shows both increases and decreases between 2021 and 2023. Some regions show slight or moderate increases, while other areas remain stable or show decreases. Stronger changes appear in localized zones rather than across the whole country. This result indicates that NO₂ change was spatially uneven and may reflect differences in urban activity, transport intensity and regional emission patterns.

Built Area Change and NO₂ Change Statistics

The built area change map identifies Stable Built Area, Built Area Gain and Built Area Loss between 2021 and 2023. Most built-up pixels are classified as stable, while gain and loss areas are more scattered and localized. The bar chart shows that Built Area Loss has the highest mean NO₂ change, approximately 1.173 µg/m³, followed by Stable Built Area and Built Area Gain. Stable Built Area also has the highest maximum value, suggesting that some persistent urban areas experienced strong localized NO₂ increases.

NO₂–Population Bivariate Exposure

The NO₂–Population bivariate map combines NO₂ concentration class and population class at the GAUL Level 2 scale. The first digit of the bivariate value represents the NO₂ class, while the second digit represents the population class. Visible combinations include 13, 14, 23 and 35, showing that regions with similar NO₂ classes can have different population exposure levels. The Class 35 area is especially important because it combines a higher NO₂ class with a high population class.

Population Exposure to NO₂ Concentration Classes

The population exposure pie chart summarizes how people are distributed across NO₂ concentration classes. The results show that 36.24% of the population is exposed to Class 1, 26.81% to Class 2 and 36.96% to Class 3. Although much of the territory is mapped as low concentration, a considerable share of the population falls into higher NO₂ exposure classes. This demonstrates why population-weighted exposure analysis is necessary in addition to simple spatial mapping.

PM2.5 Concentration in December 2023

The December 2023 PM2.5 map shows uneven concentration across Greece, with higher values around Athens and northern urban areas. Island and rural regions generally have lower particulate levels, indicating that urban emissions and transport are the main short-term drivers of PM2.5 variability.

Annual Average PM2.5 Concentration in 2023

The annual average PM2.5 map smooths seasonal variation and highlights stable spatial patterns. Higher values remain centered on large urban areas, while lower concentrations appear in less populated mainland zones and many islands.

PM2.5 Concentration Classes in 2023

The PM2.5 class map uses five health-based categories. In the 2023 result, Greece is mainly covered by Class 2 and Class 3, while Class 4 and Class 5 are absent. Class 1 areas are mainly found in island or high-altitude regions with lower particulate pollution.

PM2.5 Annual Mean Aggregation Change, 2021–2023

The PM2.5 AMAC map identifies increases and decreases between 2021 and 2023. Western Greece shows stronger increases, while coastal and eastern areas show neutral or slightly stabilizing trends.

Crop Area Change and PM2.5 Change Statistics

Crop area transitions were mapped as Stable Crop Area, Crop Gain, and Crop Loss. The PM2.5 change statistics show that Stable Crop Area has the highest mean increase, while Crop Loss shows a small mean decrease. This comparison supports the study of land-use feedbacks with particulate trends.

Crop Gain/Loss Percentage and Area Statistics

This chart shows the percentage share and absolute area of crop Gain and Loss across Greece between 2021 and 2023. Stable Crop Area still comprises the majority of cultivated land, while Gain and Loss are spatially scattered. The figure summarises the geographic directions and area magnitudes of those changes.

PM2.5–Population Bivariate Exposure

The PM2.5 bivariate map combines pollutant class and population class using a 5×5 legend. The map identifies areas where moderate pollution and high population overlap, including critical urban centers like Athens.

Population Exposure to PM2.5 Concentration Classes

The population exposure chart shows that the calculated population is concentrated in PM2.5 Class 3. This indicates that population-weighted exposure is higher than the area-based classification alone, reinforcing the need to combine spatial and demographic analysis.
